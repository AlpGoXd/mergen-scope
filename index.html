Spectrum analyser .dat exports, network analyser Touchstone files (.s1p to .sNp) and oscilloscope CSV captures. Markers, reference lines, a Smith chart, analysis and IEEE-ready figures. Your files stay on your computer.

Formats

ExtensionWhat it isWhat you get
.datSpectrum analyser exports with their headerThe trace on its real frequency axis; every header field (RBW, VBW, reference level, detector) in the file's Info table.
.datExports without a frequency columnThe axis rebuilt from the Start / Stop values in the header.
.s1p … .sNpNetwork analysers and simulators, any number of portsS, Y, Z and ABCD (2-port); dB, magnitude, phase, real, imaginary; VSWR, return loss, group delay; a Smith chart for reflections.
.csv · .txt · tab-separatedScope captures, logs, anything in columnsComma, semicolon or tab; a column wizard when the layout isn't obvious; time or frequency on X, with real units.

The file's contents decide how it is read, not the extension.

The app, working

Markers: Peak and next-peak search, delta markers measured from any marker, and reference lines that show where they cross each trace; a line can be shown in several panes.

Panes: Up to four, side by side: a chart, a table or a Smith chart. Panes with the same X unit zoom together; a time capture zooms on its own.

Analysis: Bandwidth, ripple, channel power, IP3 / TOI, noise density, stability factors, peak tables, smoothing and trace maths, as cards that recompute when you change something.

Workspaces: Save everything (files, panes, markers, analyses) to one file and open it again later, or send it to a colleague.

Figures

Export redraws the chart at its final size instead of taking a screenshot. The Paper presets work in millimetres and points: 88.9 mm for one IEEE column, 181.9 mm for two, 8 pt serif text, 0.75 pt lines, and a small symbol on each trace so it still reads in black and white. SVG stays vector; PNG goes to 600 dpi.

\end{axis}

Privacy

Mergen Scope runs entirely in your browser. When you open a file, your browser reads it; nothing is uploaded, because there is no server to upload to. The page sets no cookies and loads no trackers or third-party fonts. The only other requests it makes are for the demo files, from this site.
